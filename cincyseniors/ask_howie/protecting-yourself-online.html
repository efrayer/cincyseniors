Protecting Yourself Online

A plain-language reference guide for older adults & families — covering scams, passwords, identity theft, phone safety, and what to do if something goes wrong.

⭐ The Golden Rule

When something feels off — Stop, Verify, Protect.

  1. STOP — Don't react to pressure or urgency.
  2. VERIFY — Check through a trusted source — never through the message itself.
  3. PROTECT — Use strong passwords, MFA, updates, and backups.

💡 AI Tip: AI can help with the analysis. YOU make the final decision.

🚫 Always a Scam — No Exceptions

If any of the following happens, stop immediately — it is a scam, every single time.

Red-flag demands

  • Gift Cards / Wire Transfer — Payment demanded by gift card or wire transfer. Legitimate organizations never ask for this.
  • Password / Code Requests — Anyone asking for your password or verification code. No real company will ever ask for this.
  • Remote Access — An unsolicited request for remote access to your computer. Hang up immediately.

🔴 Never Share These

Keep these private — always

  • Passwords or verification codes
  • Full Social Security Number (last 4 digits only, if ever needed)
  • Bank or card numbers by email or text

🔑 Passwords & Accounts

  • 12+ Use 12+ Characters. Length matters more than complexity. A longer passphrase is harder to crack than a short jumble of characters.
  • 1× Never Reuse Passwords. If one account gets hacked and you reused the password, every account with that password is now at risk.
  • 🔐 Use a Password Manager. A password manager remembers all your passwords so you only need to remember one master password.
  • ✅ Turn on MFA Everywhere. Multi-factor authentication adds a second step (like a text code) that stops most account takeovers even if your password is stolen.

⚠️ Other Red Flags

  • ⏱ Urgency & Scary Language. Messages pushing you to "act now or lose your account" are designed to short-circuit your judgment. Pause.
  • ❓ Unusual Requests. Requests that seem out of character for the supposed sender — your bank, a family member, or a company.
  • 🏆 Too-Good-to-Be-True Offers. Unexpected prizes, lottery winnings, or inheritances from strangers are always scams.
  • ✏️ Typos & Odd Grammar. Legitimate companies proofread their messages. Unusual wording or spelling errors are warning signs.
  • 📧 Sender Mismatch. The display name looks familiar but the actual email address is strange — e.g., "Apple Support" sending from a Gmail address.
  • 🔗 Link Mismatch. Hover over a link (don't click!) and check that the destination matches where it claims to go.

🖥️ Protect Your Devices

  • 🔄Auto Updates. Keep your operating system and apps updated — updates fix security holes that scammers exploit.
  • 🛡️Built-In Protection. Use your device's built-in security tools. Windows Defender and macOS security are excellent starting points.
  • 💾Regular Backups. Back up your photos and important files regularly to an external drive or cloud service.
  • 🔒Lock Screen. Use a PIN, password, or fingerprint to lock your phone and computer when not in use.
  • 📍Find My Device. Enable "Find My" (Apple) or "Find My Device" (Android/Windows) so you can locate or remotely wipe a lost device.

🪪 Your Identity

  • 🗂️Shred Documents. Shred anything with account numbers, Social Security numbers, or personal details before throwing it away.
  • 📬Bring In Your Mail Daily. Mail left in the box is an easy target for identity thieves.
  • 🏦Review Statements Regularly. Check bank and credit card statements for unfamiliar charges at least once a month.
  • ❄️Consider a Credit Freeze. Freezing your credit at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion is free and prevents anyone from opening new credit in your name.

📱 Phone & Wi-Fi Safety

  • 📵Let Unknown Numbers Go to Voicemail. If it's important, they'll leave a message. Scammers rarely do.
  • ✋Don't Tap Links in Texts. Even if the text looks like it's from a familiar company — FedEx, your bank, the USPS — go directly to their official website instead.
  • 📲Official App Stores Only. Only install apps from the official Apple App Store or Google Play Store.
  • 📶Avoid Banking on Public Wi-Fi. Public Wi-Fi at coffee shops or airports isn't secure. Use your phone's cellular hotspot for sensitive tasks.

👨‍👩‍👧 The Family Emergency Scam

AI can now clone a loved one's voice convincingly.

  • If a caller claims a family member is in trouble and needs money sent immediately — do not trust the voice alone.
  • Ask for your family's secret word (agree on one in advance).
  • Hang up and call the person back on a number you already know.

🆘 If You Think You've Been Scammed

  • 1Stop the conversation — hang up, close the window, or leave the chat immediately.
  • 2Change your passwords — starting with email, then banking, then everything else.
  • 3Call your bank — immediately if any money was moved or financial details were shared.
  • 4Save the details — take screenshots or write down what happened for reporting purposes.

🤖 How to Check a Suspicious Message

  • 📸 AI Second Opinion. Screenshot the message, cover any private details (name, address, account numbers), then paste it into an AI chatbot and ask "Is this a scam?"
  • 🔍 Free Scam-Check Tools. Norton Genie (genie.norton.com) and Bitdefender Scamio (scamio.bitdefender.com) — both free, both fast.
