Help Paying for Prescription Medicines

An A-to-Z guide to foundations, drug company programs, discount cards, and government help — compiled by Howie Baum and AI, November 2026.

⭐ Where to Start

Four steps to find the right help quickly

  1. Find out who makes your medicine. The company that makes it often has a free-medicine program (see Part 3 below). Your pharmacist can tell you the maker.
  2. If you have Medicare, check Part 4 first. Your drug costs are capped each year, and Extra Help can lower them much further.
  3. If you have insurance but can't afford the copay, try the charitable foundations in Part 1, and sign up for FundFinder alerts for funds that are closed.
  4. For generic drugs, compare cash prices with the discount cards and low-cost pharmacies in Part 2.

Programs, income limits and open disease funds change often. Check the website or call before applying. Parts and programs are listed in alphabetical order.

1. Charitable Foundations That Help Pay Copays

Independent nonprofits that pay copays, coinsurance and sometimes premiums for people who have insurance — including Medicare — but cannot afford their share. Help is organized into "disease funds" that open and close as money comes in, so check back if a fund is closed. Most have income limits.

  • aAccessia Health (formerly Patient Services, Inc.)
    📞 800-366-7741accessiahealth.org
    Copay and premium help for people with certain chronic and rare conditions.
  • bBlood Cancer United Co-Pay Assistance Program (formerly the Leukemia and Lymphoma Society)
    📞 877-557-2672bloodcancerunited.org
    Help with drug copays, labs and scans for people with a blood cancer.
  • cBrain Tumor Drug Copayment Assistance Program (Musella Foundation)
    📞 855-426-2672braintumorcopays.org
    Copay help for brain tumor medicines.
  • dCancerCare Co-Payment Assistance Foundation
    📞 866-552-6729 (866-55-COPAY)cancercare.org/copayfoundation
    Copay help for cancer medicines.
  • eGood Days (formerly Chronic Disease Fund)
    📞 877-968-7233mygooddays.org
    Copay help, plus some travel help, for chronic diseases and cancers. Income limit about 500% of the poverty level.
  • fHealthWell Foundation
    📞 800-675-8416healthwellfoundation.org
    One of the largest foundations; pays copays, coinsurance, deductibles and premiums for many chronic and life-altering illnesses.
  • gNational Organization for Rare Disorders (NORD)
    📞 800-999-6673rarediseases.org
    Assistance programs for people with rare diseases; phone numbers vary by program.
  • hPatient Advocate Foundation TotalAssist (now includes the former PAN Foundation)
    📞 866-512-3861totalassist.org
    The PAN Foundation merged into Patient Advocate Foundation in 2026, now one program called TotalAssist with more than 140 disease funds. Free case managers also available: 800-532-5274.
  • iThe Assistance Fund (TAF)
    📞 855-845-3663tafcares.org
    Copay, coinsurance and deductible help for many life-threatening, chronic and rare diseases.

2. Discount Cards and Low-Cost Pharmacies

These are not insurance. They can save a lot on generic drugs or for people without drug coverage. People with Medicare can use them, but what they pay this way usually does not count toward the Medicare Part D yearly limit — so compare prices first.

  • aGoodRx
    📱 Online and appgoodrx.com
    Free coupons that show the lowest cash price at nearby pharmacies.
  • bMark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company
    📱 Online onlycostplusdrugs.com
    Mail-order pharmacy that sells many generic drugs at cost plus a small markup.
  • cRx Outreach
    📱 Onlinerxoutreach.org
    Nonprofit mail-order pharmacy with more than 950 low-cost medicine strengths. Open to people with or without insurance; no membership fee.
  • dSingleCare
    📱 Online and appsinglecare.com
    Free pharmacy discount card and price comparison.
  • eWellRx (ScriptSave)
    📞 800-407-8156wellrx.com
    Free pharmacy discount card and price comparison.

3. Drug Company Free-Medicine Programs

Most large drug companies give their own brand-name medicines free to people who meet income limits, usually for 12 months at a time with yearly renewal. Many also accept people with Medicare Part D who still cannot afford their drugs. Start with the company that makes your medicine; your doctor's office usually has to sign the application.

  • aAbbVie: myAbbVie Assist
    📞 800-222-6885abbvie.com/patients
    Free AbbVie medicines such as Humira, Rinvoq and Skyrizi for people who qualify.
  • bAmgen Safety Net Foundation
    📞 888-762-6436amgensafetynetfoundation.com
    Free Amgen medicines such as Enbrel and Repatha for people who qualify.
  • cAstraZeneca: AZ&Me Prescription Savings Program
    📞 800-292-6363azandmeapp.com
    Free AstraZeneca medicines such as Farxiga, Symbicort and Brilinta, including programs for people with Medicare Part D.
  • dBoehringer Ingelheim Cares Foundation (BI Cares)
    📞 800-556-8317boehringer-ingelheim.com/us
    Free Boehringer medicines such as Jardiance, Tradjenta, Spiriva and Ofev for uninsured and underinsured people who meet income limits.
  • eBristol Myers Squibb Patient Assistance Foundation
    📞 800-736-0003bmspaf.org
    Free BMS medicines such as Eliquis and Orencia for people who qualify.
  • fEli Lilly: Lilly Cares Foundation
    📞 800-545-6962lillycares.com
    Free Lilly medicines, including insulin and Trulicity, for up to 12 months at a time.
  • gGenentech Patient Foundation
    📞 See websitegene.com
    Free Genentech medicines for people without insurance or whose insurance denied coverage.
  • hGSK Patient Assistance Program
    📞 866-728-4368gskforyou.com
    Free GSK medicines such as Trelegy, Breo and Advair for people who qualify.
  • iJohnson & Johnson Patient Assistance Foundation
    📞 800-652-6227jjpaf.org
    Free J&J medicines such as Xarelto and Stelara for uninsured people who qualify.
  • jMerck Helps (Merck Patient Assistance Program)
    📞 800-727-5400merckhelps.com
    Free Merck medicines such as Januvia and Janumet; accepts some people with Medicare.
  • kNovartis Patient Assistance Foundation
    📞 800-277-2254pap.novartis.com
    Free Novartis medicines such as Entresto for people who qualify.
  • lNovo Nordisk Patient Assistance Program (NovoCare)
    📞 866-310-7549novocare.com
    Free Novo Nordisk insulins and diabetes medicines for people who qualify.
  • mPfizer RxPathways
    📞 844-989-7284pfizerrxpathways.com
    Free or low-cost Pfizer medicines; covers hundreds of Pfizer products.
  • nSanofi Patient Connection
    📞 888-847-4877sanofipatientconnection.com
    Free Sanofi medicines, including Lantus and other insulins, for people who qualify.
  • oTakeda Help at Hand
    📞 800-830-9159takeda.com
    Help for people with no or too little insurance who need Takeda medicines.
  • pTeva Cares Foundation
    📞 See websitetevausa.com
    Certain Teva medicines at no cost for people who meet insurance and income rules.

4. Government Programs (Medicare and Medicaid)

For people with Medicare, these are the biggest money-savers — and several happen automatically.

  • aExtra Help (Medicare Part D Low-Income Subsidy)
    📞 800-772-1213 (Social Security)ssa.gov/extrahelp
    For people with limited income and savings. Lowers or removes Part D premiums and deductibles, and limits copays in 2026 to about $5.10 for a generic and $12.65 for a brand-name drug.
  • bMedicaid (Ohio, Kentucky and other states)
    📞 Ohio: 844-640-6446benefits.ohio.gov
    Full drug coverage for people with low income. Anyone with Medicaid and Medicare gets Extra Help automatically.
  • cMedicare Part D Yearly Limit on Drug Costs
    📞 800-633-4227 (1-800-MEDICARE)medicare.gov
    In 2026, nobody with a Medicare drug plan pays more than $2,100 a year out of pocket for covered drugs; it rises to $2,400 in 2027. This is automatic — premiums do not count toward it.
  • dMedicare Prescription Payment Plan
    📞 Call your drug planmedicare.gov/prescription-payment-plan
    Lets you spread your drug costs into monthly payments instead of paying large amounts at the pharmacy — especially helpful early in the year.
  • eMedicare's List of Drug Company Programs
    📱 Onlinemedicare.gov/pharmaceutical-assistance-program
    Searchable by medicine name; shows which company programs accept people with Medicare.
  • fState Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP)
    📞 877-839-2675  ·  Ohio OSHIIP: 800-686-1578shiphelp.org
    Free, unbiased counselors in every state who help compare Medicare drug plans each fall and check eligibility for Extra Help.
  • gState Pharmaceutical Assistance Programs
    📱 Onlinencsl.org
    Some states have their own drug help programs for older adults. The National Conference of State Legislatures keeps a state-by-state list.

5. Other Countries

If you have family or dual citizenship in another country, prescription costs may be dramatically lower. Here are three examples:

  • aAustralia: Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS)
    health.gov.au
    Since January 1, 2026, a PBS prescription costs no more than A$25, or A$7.70 with a concession card. After a yearly "Safety Net" amount, medicines get even cheaper or free.
  • bCanada: Provincial Drug Plans
    canada.ca
    Each province runs its own drug plan, and most cover adults 65 and older. For example, the Ontario Drug Benefit covers most Ontario residents 65+.
  • cUnited Kingdom: NHS Prescriptions
    nhs.uk
    In England, prescriptions are free for everyone 60 and over. In Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, prescriptions are free for everyone.

6. Search Tools That Find Programs for You

These free websites search hundreds of programs at once. Type in the medicine name to see every program that might help.

  • aAmerica's Medicines (formerly the Medicine Assistance Tool)
    📱 Onlinemat.org
    Run by the drug industry group PhRMA. Searches drug company programs by medicine, insurance, income and location, and also finds free and low-cost clinics.
  • bBenefitsCheckUp (National Council on Aging)
    📱 Onlinebenefitscheckup.org
    Answer a few questions to find drug, food, utility and other benefit programs for people 55 and older.
  • cFundFinder (Patient Advocate Foundation)
    📱 Onlinepatientadvocate.org
    Free alerts by email or text the moment a charitable disease fund for your condition reopens.
  • dNeedyMeds
    📞 800-503-6897needymeds.org
    Nonprofit with the largest free database of drug company programs, coupons, free clinics and a free drug discount card.
  • eRxAssist
    📱 Onlinerxassist.org
    Nonprofit database of drug company programs, with current application forms to print.
